Laryngectomy Surgery

What is Laryngectomy Surgery?

Laryngectomy surgery involves the removal of the larynx or voice box. It is commonly recommended for patients with laryngeal cancer or advanced throat cancer. The surgery may be total (removal of the entire larynx) or partial (removal of a portion), depending on the size and location of the tumor. This procedure is crucial to prevent cancer spread and restore patient health.

Types of Laryngectomy Surgery

  • Total Laryngectomy: Complete removal of the larynx, often recommended for advanced laryngeal cancer.

  • Partial Laryngectomy: Only part of the larynx is removed to preserve some voice function.

  • Voice Box Removal Surgery: Focused on removing the affected portion of the larynx while maintaining breathing pathways.

Why Laryngectomy Surgery is Performed

  • Treat laryngeal cancer or throat cancer effectively.

  • Remove tumors that cannot be treated with radiation or chemotherapy alone.

  • Prevent cancer from spreading to nearby organs and tissues.

Symptoms for Laryngectomy Surgery

  • Persistent hoarseness or voice changes

  • Difficulty swallowing or breathing

  • Chronic throat pain

  • Presence of tumors or growths in the larynx

  • Unexplained coughing or blood in saliva

Risks and Complications

  • Bleeding or infection at the surgical site

  • Difficulty swallowing or speaking initially

  • Changes in taste or sensation in the throat

  • Need for temporary feeding tube or tracheostomy

  • Rare complications like airway obstruction

Benefits of Laryngectomy Surgery

  • Removes cancer effectively and improves survival chances

  • Prevents spread of laryngeal or throat tumors

  • Enables voice rehabilitation with prosthesis

  • Reduces long-term health risks associated with untreated cancer

  • Enhances quality of life after recovery

How the Surgery is Done (Procedure Overview)

  1. Pre-operative evaluation including imaging and lab tests

  2. Anesthesia is administered for a pain-free procedure

  3. Surgeon removes all or part of the larynx depending on tumor size

  4. Tracheostomy is performed to maintain breathing

  5. Voice rehabilitation using voice prosthesis may be planned

  6. Surgical site is closed and patient monitored in post-op care

Recovery and Post-Operative Care

  • Hospital stay: Usually 7–10 days for monitoring and initial recovery

  • Pain management, antibiotics, and wound care are provided

  • Post-laryngectomy care includes learning breathing techniques, voice rehabilitation, and dietary adjustments

  • Regular follow-ups to monitor healing and prevent recurrence

Life After Laryngectomy

  • Patients may use voice prosthesis to regain speech

  • Breathing, swallowing, and eating routines may require adaptation

  • Emotional support and counseling are encouraged

  • With proper care, patients can lead a normal, fulfilling life
